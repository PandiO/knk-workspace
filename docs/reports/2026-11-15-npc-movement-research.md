# NPC movement: how games handle obstacle detection, avoidance and stuck recovery — research report

**Status:** Research for developer question 2 (steering) of the movement-quality design · **Last updated:** 2026-11-15

**Issue:** [PandiO/knk-workspace#603](https://github.com/PandiO/knk-workspace/issues/603) (link L8 of the [NPC navigation follow-ups chain](../ai-agents/handoffs/NPC_NAVIGATION_FOLLOWUPS_CHAIN.md)).
**Design:** [implementation plan Phase 4c](../specs/npc-platform/IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md#phase-4c--movement-quality-steering-formation-keeping-crowding-plaza-lines-plugin-follow-ups), [BEHAVIOR_MODEL § 2](../specs/npc-platform/BEHAVIOR_MODEL.md#navigation-adapter-requirements-from-the-spike-2026-11-11) NAV-24–NAV-28.
**Why:** the developer, 2026-11-15: "I want to go over this in detail. Specifically how other games handle this in the industry. Please research how other systems handle npc's obstacle detection and its behaviour to avoid recover from it. I feel like with the help of examples and techniques already used (best practices) in the field, we can easily improve, since we are working with a relatively simple 3d grid."
**Method:** web search of primary sources where they exist (papers, engine documentation and source headers, the authors' own articles) and secondary sources (forum threads, community write-ups) where they don't; each claim names its source, and secondary sources are marked as such. The K&K side is read on knk-plugin `next` at `8ad9df66`.

## 1. Summary

1. **Nearly every engine splits movement into the same layers**: a global plan (a graph or mesh search), a **path corridor** that is straightened (string pulling), **local steering** that looks a short way ahead and avoids walls and other agents, and **locomotion** (the body). Reynolds named three of these layers in 1999; Recast/Detour, Unity and Unreal ship them as separate parts. K&K has the global plan (the road graph) and locomotion (Citizens), but the middle two layers are thin: the line is the road skeleton as is, and local steering is Citizens walking straight from waypoint to waypoint.
2. **Agent size is baked into the walkable map, not detected at run time.** Recast erodes the walkable area by the agent's radius before it builds the mesh; clearance-based grid search (HAA*) stores per cell how big an agent fits there. Afterwards a path through the map cannot graze a wall. K&K checks the body's width only on some steps (`NavGeometry.straightWalkable`) and keeps clearance only at turns (`CornerClearance`). This is the most direct lesson for the jagged-wall spots.
3. **Agents look at the farthest corner they can see, not the next node**: Detour's corridor gives the next few corners and is re-optimised while the agent walks; the funnel algorithm and any-angle search (Theta*) produce lines with few turns. On a block grid, line-of-sight checks against the clearance map are cheap.
4. **Agent-to-agent avoidance (RVO, ORCA, Detour's velocity sampling) prevents collisions but is known to deadlock in doorways and narrow corridors**; papers and engine forums report it, and the fixes are global ones: priorities, turn-taking, temporarily switching avoidance off, or a small multi-agent search at the chokepoint. Since K&K's bodies don't collide (`collidable: false`), turn-taking at chokepoints gives the visible benefit for less cost.
5. **Stuck handling in practice is a progress watchdog plus an escalating ladder**: Minecraft's own navigation stops a mob that hasn't moved enough between checks; Citizens cancels after `stationaryTicks` and runs a stuck action (teleport by default); Unreal reports a `Blocked` path result; NextBot has an explicit stuck state with a handler; community code escalates side-step → back-step → jump → teleport. K&K's L3 ladder (back-off → side-step → local leg → replan) is already this pattern, without the teleport.
6. **Groups**: RTS games plan once per group, move the group at the slowest member's speed, and let units split and merge around obstacles (Pottinger, Age of Empires); flow fields (Supreme Commander 2, Planetary Annihilation) suit many units with one goal; flocking (StarCraft II) suits loose groups. None of the sources describes a formation that narrows with look-ahead in detail; the ones that do it (Total War) are criticised for squeezing formations through chokepoints badly.
7. **Options for question 2** (section 5): (A) shape the waypoint line inside a clearance map per stretch and let Citizens walk it; (B) A plus a short-horizon K&K steering layer that re-aims every few ticks; (C) a K&K step controller that steers the body every tick. A clearance-aware K&K grid search for local legs and recovery (D) fits under any of them. The developer decides.

## 2. What K&K does today

Read on knk-plugin `next` `8ad9df66`; details in [plan 4c.1](../specs/npc-platform/IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md#4c1-what-the-code-does-today).

1. **Global plan:** the road graph (knk-core `core/roads/`), built from a road mask by a distance transform and Zhang-Suen thinning (the skeleton is the medial axis, the middle of the road), simplified by Ramer-Douglas-Peucker; routes by A* on the graph (`AStarRouter`).
2. **Corridor:** none. The NPC's line is the route's polyline, resampled every 1.5 blocks (`RouteFollower`), each point snapped to its floor (`HopPlanner.surfaceNear`), turn points pushed off the inside wall by `corner-clearance` (`CornerClearance`, turns of 30° or more), and every step bridged so it is a straight walk on floor (`WaypointChain`, L5).
3. **Local steering:** Citizens walks the waypoint list in straight lines, moving to the next waypoint within `waypoint-margin` (0.5). It sees nothing beside the line. Citizens' own A* is used only for short local legs and recovery.
4. **Stuck handling:** Citizens' stationary check (`stationary-ticks` 40) and K&K's progress watchdog (`no-progress-ticks` 40, NAV-17), then the recovery ladder (L3, `RecoveryLadder`): back-off along the NPC's own footsteps, side-step away from the blocking face, a local leg ahead on its own floor, a replan; spaced (`recovery-wait-ticks`) and capped (`max-retries`). Citizens' teleport is switched off (`NO_TELEPORT`).
5. **Other agents:** none. Bodies don't collide (`collidable: false`); groups have slots and lanes (L4), and followers stopped at a door queue a spacing apart.
6. **Budget:** 0.0021 ms per NPC per tick for K&K's navigation on the main thread (Phase 4); the chain's budget is about 0.03 ms.

## 3. Survey

### 3.1 Minecraft's own mob navigation

1. **How it works.** Vanilla mobs search a path over block nodes with A* (`PathFinder`); a node evaluator decides what each block means for the mob: the ground evaluator `WalkNodeEvaluator` classifies blocks into path types (open, blocked, fence, door, danger, water and so on, each with a cost malus) and tests the mob's bounding box against them, with a cache of path types and collisions per position ([`WalkNodeEvaluator`, mappings.dev](https://mappings.dev/1.19.1/net/minecraft/world/level/pathfinder/WalkNodeEvaluator.html); a walk-through in Chinese: [bilibili](https://www.bilibili.com/read/cv23090029/)). `PathNavigation` then follows the node list.
2. **Stuck handling.** `PathNavigation` has `STUCK_CHECK_INTERVAL`, `STUCK_THRESHOLD_DISTANCE_FACTOR`, `lastStuckCheckPos` and `isStuck` ([mappings.dev, 25w10a](https://mappings.dev/25w10a/net/minecraft/world/entity/ai/navigation/PathNavigation.html)). `doStuckDetection` checks whether the mob has moved enough since the last check and stops the navigation if not ([javadoc, 26.1.x](https://aldak.netlify.app/javadoc/26.1.x/net/minecraft/world/entity/ai/navigation/pathnavigation)); there is also a per-node timeout (`checkTimeouts`, `timeoutPath`, `resetStuckTimeout`, [mappings.dev, 1.18](https://mappings.dev/1.18/net/minecraft/world/entity/ai/navigation/PathNavigation.html)). Paper skips point-of-interest searches for mobs whose navigation `isStuck()` ([Paper patch](https://ayakael.net/mirrors/papermc/src/commit/862299b6a6588ff60db69dbc40e8514f9690d318/patches/server/0930-Skip-POI-finding-if-stuck-in-vehicle.patch)). The exact interval and threshold values were not in the sources found; check the decompiled source of the server version before quoting numbers.
3. **What it teaches.** The body's box is part of the node test (agent size baked into the search), stuck detection is a slow progress check, and the reaction is simply to stop and let the mob's goal plan again: recovery lives one level up.
4. **For K&K.** Citizens' A* and its examiners play this role for K&K's local legs. Vanilla mobs wander and chase; they don't walk long authored routes in formation, so the vanilla navigation is not a model for road following.

### 3.2 Baritone (community pathfinder)

1. **How it works.** Baritone, a client-side bot, runs A* over block movements with costs in ticks. Because it can't see beyond the loaded area it computes in **segments**: a search ends at the goal, on a time-out or at the edge of loaded chunks, and then picks the best node under increasing cost coefficients that is at least 5 blocks from the start; it ignores alternatives that improve the cost by less than 0.01 ticks, and the search runs on its own thread while the game thread reads the best path so far ([Baritone FEATURES.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cabaletta/baritone/1.19.4/FEATURES.md)).
2. **What it teaches.** Plan in segments ahead of the body, keep planning off the main thread, and cost movements in time (stairs, jumps and doors cost more).
3. **For K&K.** K&K already plans the road once (no chunks needed) and hands over stretches early (NAV-14); Baritone's segment search is closest to K&K's off-road hops and local legs. It is built for one player bot, not hundreds of NPCs.

### 3.3 Citizens

1. **How it works.** Citizens' navigator walks to a target or a list of waypoints with its own A* or Minecraft's (the "new finder"). `NavigatorParameters` hold, among others, `stationaryTicks` ("the number of stationary ticks before navigation is cancelled") and `stuckAction`, which "will be run when the navigation is stuck and must either be fixed up or cancelled"; default parameters are copied into per-target local parameters when a target starts ([NavigatorParameters javadoc](https://jd.citizensnpcs.co/net/citizensnpcs/api/ai/NavigatorParameters.html)). The default stuck action teleports the NPC; a known 1.18-era issue had NPCs teleporting instead of walking, with the new finder as a workaround ([Citizens2 #2752](https://www.github.com/CitizensDev/Citizens2/issues/2752)).
2. **What it teaches.** The engine K&K uses has the watchdog-plus-action pattern built in, with teleport as its default fix.
3. **For K&K.** K&K replaced the teleport with its own ladder (`NO_TELEPORT`) and uses `distanceMargin` per waypoint list (L3). Citizens offers no look-ahead or avoidance beside the line when walking a waypoint list; that has to come from what K&K hands it.

### 3.4 Recast and Detour (navigation meshes)

1. **Building the walkable area.** Recast voxelises the level, keeps spans an agent can stand on, and then **erodes** the walkable area by the agent's radius: "spans closer to a boundary or obstruction than the specified radius are marked as unwalkable" ([`rcErodeWalkableArea`](https://docs.unrealengine.com/4.27/API/Runtime/Navmesh/Recast/rcErodeWalkableArea/index.html); `walkableRadius`, "the distance to erode/shrink the walkable area of the heightfield away from obstructions", [rcConfig](https://docs.unrealengine.com/4.26/en-US/API/Runtime/Navmesh/Recast/rcConfig/walkableRadius/index.html)). The erosion is computed on a distance field. Known drawback: it is symmetric, so it also shrinks the area next to drops, and narrow doors can close; a maintainer suggests cells at most half the agent radius ([Recast group thread](https://groups.google.com/g/recastnavigation/c/k3jAZFAisDE); [margins along ledges, Unreal forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/nav-mesh-margins-along-ledges-can-they-be-removed/321206)).
2. **Straightening.** A path is a corridor of polygons; the **funnel algorithm** (string pulling) gives the shortest line through it. Mikko Mononen's "simple stupid funnel algorithm" restarts from the last corner instead of keeping a queue ([Digesting Duck, 2010](https://backiee.wasmer.app/https_digestingduck_blogspot_com/2010/03/simple-stupid-funnel-algorithm_html)); a user reports that the simplified version can give illegal paths in some cases ([jvm-gaming.org](https://jvm-gaming.org/t/simple-stupid-funnel-algorithm/55185)).
3. **Local steering (DetourCrowd).** Each agent keeps its own path corridor and a local boundary (the nearby walls), and the corridor is re-optimised from time to time by a local search ("topology optimisation") and by visibility checks; agents steer to the next corners, and a velocity-sampling obstacle avoidance (`dtObstacleAvoidanceQuery`, adaptive sampling with rings and divisions, scored by desired velocity, current velocity, side and time to impact) picks a velocity that avoids neighbours and wall segments, limited by maximum acceleration ([DetourCrowd docs](https://recastnav.com/group__crowd.html); [DetourCrowd.h](https://recastnav.com/DetourCrowd_8h.html); [dtObstacleAvoidanceParams](https://docs.unrealengine.com/4.26/API/Runtime/Navmesh/DetourCrowd/dtObstacleAvoidanceParams/index.html)).
4. **Where it fails.** Large crowds get stuck at corners; one user's workaround changes `collisionQueryRange` when an agent's velocity drops below a threshold ([recastnavigation discussion #675](https://www.github.com/recastnavigation/recastnavigation/discussions/675)); agents may need to steer outside the corridor on curved paths ([Digesting Duck, 2010](https://digestingduck.blogspot.com/2010/07/constrained-movement-along-navmesh-pt-3.html)).
5. **For K&K.** The road graph is K&K's coarse plan; what K&K lacks is Recast's eroded walkable area and the corridor/funnel step. On a block grid both are simple: a distance transform over a strip of cells around the road line (K&K's road builder already has `DistanceTransform` for the road mask) and line-of-sight checks against it. Erosion must be soft for K&K: a block world has one-block doors that a 0.6-wide body passes, so a hard 0.8 clearance would close them.

### 3.5 Unity and Unreal navigation agents

1. **Unity.** `NavMeshAgent` has a radius (used for collisions with other objects), an obstacle-avoidance quality (lower is cheaper; "none" still resolves collisions but doesn't actively avoid), and a priority from 0 to 99 (agents avoid higher-priority agents and ignore lower ones) ([Unity manual](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.ai.navigation@1.1/manual/NavMeshAgent)). Community workarounds for stuck agents: turn an idle agent into an obstacle, or detect a stuck agent and switch its avoidance off or change its priority for a while ([Unity discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/official-way-to-make-agents-avoid-each-other/739021)); these are anecdotal.
2. **Unreal.** RVO avoidance lives in the character movement and ignores the navigation mesh, so it can push agents off it; the Detour Crowd manager does avoidance by adaptive velocity sampling on the mesh ([Epic docs, avoidance](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/using-avoidance-with-the-navigation-system-in-unreal-engine?lang=ko); [forum: RVO pushing AI off the navmesh](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/rvo-avoidance-causing-ai-to-get-stuck-and-pushing-them-out-of-navmeshes-issue/313611)). Path following reports a `Blocked` result ([Navigation API](https://docs.unrealengine.com/4.27/API/Runtime/AIModule/Navigation/index.html)); users report twitchy, constantly correcting crowd agents that still get stuck ([forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/how-can-i-improve-my-crowd-detour-behavior-characters-constantly-correct-their-path-looks-twitchy-and-weird-and-often-get-stuck/273131)).
3. **For K&K.** Two lessons: priorities break symmetric standoffs, and avoidance that ignores the walkable map pushes agents into walls. K&K's turn order at chokepoints (plan 4c.3 decision 3) is a priority rule; any K&K steering must stay inside the clearance map.

### 3.6 Steering behaviours (Reynolds) and context steering

1. **How they work.** Reynolds' 1999 paper splits motion into action selection, steering and locomotion, and defines seek, flee, arrival, obstacle avoidance, containment, wall following, path following, flow-field following, unaligned collision avoidance, leader following, and the flocking rules separation, cohesion and alignment ([Reynolds 1999](https://ics-websites.science.uu.nl/docs/vakken/mcrws/papers_new/Reynolds%20-%201999%20-%20Steering%20behaviors%20for%20autonomous%20characters.pdf)). Path following projects the agent's future position onto the path and seeks the nearest point; wall following uses the nearest wall point and its normal ([course slides](https://diana.ms.mff.cuni.cz/pogamut_files/lectures/2015-2016/steeringBehaviors-slides.pdf)).
2. **Where they fail.** Summed steering forces cancel out (an obstacle straight ahead on the path) and oscillate in corridors. **Context steering** (Andrew Fray, Game AI Pro 2, chapter 18) fixes this: each behaviour writes an interest map and a danger map over a fixed set of headings, dangerous headings are masked, and the best remaining heading wins ([Game AI Pro 2, ch. 18](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/game-ai-pro/9781482254792/K23980_C018.xhtml); [Lewis, gamedev.net](https://gamedev.net/articles/programming/artificial-intelligence/smarter-steering-using-context-for-direction-r3149)).
3. **For K&K.** Steering needs control of the body's heading every few ticks; with Citizens that means handing it a short, frequently replaced target, or a step controller (section 5, options B and C). On a block grid a context map over 8 or 16 headings against the clearance map is a handful of cached look-ups.

### 3.7 Velocity obstacles, RVO and ORCA

1. **How they work.** Each agent picks a velocity outside the velocity obstacles of its neighbours, sharing the avoidance ("reciprocal"); ORCA solves it as a small linear program per agent.
2. **Where they fail.** Several papers report that ORCA gets stuck in narrow passages: "agents approaching nearly at the same time will be stuck", and the reciprocal approach "usually cannot avoid or resolve deadlock"; one fix combines ORCA with Theta* and a local multi-agent search ("Push and Rotate") when a deadlock pattern appears, reported at 95 % success in single-passage maps where plain ORCA failed ([Dergachev et al., arXiv 2008.01227](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2008.01227); [survey, arXiv 2508.13459](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.13459)).
3. **For K&K.** ORCA needs per-tick velocity control of every agent and fails exactly where K&K's developer saw the jam (a doorway). It solves collisions between bodies; K&K's bodies don't collide. A turn-taking rule at chokepoints is the deadlock fix those papers add on top anyway.

### 3.8 RTS movement: flow fields, groups and formations

1. **Flow fields.** Supreme Commander 2 and Planetary Annihilation use flow fields: a high-level search over sectors, then a flow field per goal over the sectors on that path, built in tiles to save memory and time, with line-of-sight tests to the destination near it ([Emerson, "Crowd Pathfinding and Steering Using Flow Field Tiles", Game AI Pro](https://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter23_Crowd_Pathfinding_and_Steering_Using_Flow_Field_Tiles.pdf); summary: [howtorts](https://howtorts.github.io/2014/01/04/basic-flow-fields.html); secondary: [gamedev.net](https://gamedev.net/forums/topic/701267-flow-field-architecture-advice/5401826/)). Strong for many units with one goal and for mazes ([Red Blob Games](https://redblobgames.com/blog/2024-04-27-flow-field-pathfinding)).
2. **StarCraft II.** Units mostly steer themselves with flocking-like behaviour; units avoid each other instead of packing as tightly as collision allows, which gives the clumpier movement, and the designers favoured predictable behaviour (secondary: [gamedev.net thread on SC2 pathfinding](https://gamedev.net/forums/topic/563819-whats-starcraft2s-pathfinding-tech/), [SBGames 2021 paper](https://www.sbgames.org/proceedings2021/ComputacaoFull/217610.pdf)). The GDC 2011 talk by James Anhalt is the primary source and was not read here.
3. **Age of Empires.** Dave Pottinger's two articles (1999) argue that executing a path matters as much as finding it, and cover group movement and its hard cases ([Coordinated Unit Movement](https://gamedeveloper.com/programming/coordinated-unit-movement); [Implementing Coordinated Movement](https://gamedeveloper.com/programming/implementing-coordinated-movement)); a formation moves at its slowest unit's speed so it stays together ([openage notes](https://simonsan.github.io/openage-webdocs/sphinx/doc/reverse_engineering/game_mechanics/formations.html)). Pottinger's GDC talk on pathing in Age of Empires is in the GDC Vault ([GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1027659/Pathing-in-Age-of-Empires)).
4. **Formations at chokepoints.** Total War players criticise formations sprinting through chokepoints unchanged ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/workshop/filedetails/discussion/362473569/4357871368303663075)); 0 A.D. developers discuss splitting a big formation into smaller ones that pass one at a time and re-form ([Wildfire Games forum](https://wildfiregames.com/forum/topic/15363-formation-pathfinding-was-analysing-performance-problems/?page=4); [formation pathfinding proposal](https://trac.wildfiregames.com/raw-attachment/ticket/3280/formation_pathfinding.pdf)). No primary source found on a formation that narrows with look-ahead.
5. **For K&K.** A K&K group already plans once and walks slots on the leader's path. The lessons that carry over: shape changes before a chokepoint, splitting into sub-groups that pass in turn, and a slowest-member pace. Flow fields don't fit K&K's many routes with their own goals, but a small local field around one chokepoint (a doorway queue) is cheap.

### 3.9 Grid techniques

1. **Clearance-based search (HAA*).** Each cell stores its clearance (the largest agent that fits there); a cell is usable when the agent's size fits its clearance and its terrain is allowed. True clearance is complete for any agent size, and one abstract graph serves agents of different sizes ([Harabor and Botea, CIG 2008](https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/clearance-based-pathfinding-and-hierarchical-annotated-a-search/); [Harabor's pathfinding page](https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~dharabor/pathfinding.html)).
2. **Jump Point Search.** Symmetry breaking for optimal grid search: neighbours are replaced by jump points further away; variants forbid corner cutting, JPS+ precomputes jump points, and a 3D version keeps feasibility and optimality ([Harabor and Grastien, the JPS system](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/SOCS/article/view/18254); [Improving JPS, ICAPS 2014](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/view/13633)).
3. **HPA*.** A grid split into clusters with precomputed crossing costs; search at the cluster level, then refine; up to 10 times faster than optimised A* with paths within 1 % of optimal ([Botea, Müller and Schaeffer 2004](https://webdocs.cs.ualberta.ca/%7emmueller/ps/hpastar.pdf)).
4. **Any-angle search (Theta*).** A* whose parent links may skip grid edges when there is line of sight, giving shorter, more natural paths; Lazy Theta* needs about an order of magnitude fewer line-of-sight checks in 3D ([Daniel, Nash, Koenig and Felner, JAIR 2010](https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/10676); [Lazy Theta*](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/SOCS/article/view/18152)).
5. **Distance fields and string pulling.** A distance transform gives every cell its distance to the nearest obstacle (Recast's erosion, HAA*'s clearance and K&K's road skeleton all come from one); the funnel algorithm straightens a path through a corridor (3.4 item 2).
6. **For K&K.** K&K's road graph is already a hierarchical plan (HPA* in spirit), so JPS and HPA* add little for roads. Clearance per cell, line-of-sight on it, and Theta*-style smoothing are the cheap wins for the local line, for plazas and for local legs.

### 3.10 Stuck detection and recovery in practice

1. **Progress watchdogs.** Check every so often whether an agent that should move has moved far enough; if not, it is stuck (Minecraft, 3.1; Citizens' `stationaryTicks`, 3.3; an Unreal suggestion to check one controller per tick against a distance threshold, [forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/detect-is-ai-has-become-stuck-during-moveto/76171)).
2. **Engine stuck states.** Source's NextBot locomotion has `IsStuck()`, a `HandleStuck` handler, `ClearStuck()` and an `OnUnStuck` hook; the example path loop calls the handler and abandons the path ([Garry's Mod wiki, NextBot:HandleStuck](https://wiki.facepunch.com/gmod/NextBot:HandleStuck); [CLuaLocomotion:IsStuck](https://wiki.facepunch.com/gmod/CLuaLocomotion:IsStuck)).
3. **Escalating unstuck manoeuvres.** A community example escalates side-step and back-step, then a jump with a cooldown, then a teleport to a nearby safe spot ([Roblox devforum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/system-to-know-when-pathfinding-ai-is-stuck-in-a-wall/1514936)); a guide recommends a repath timer and keeping the agent radius inside the walkable area ([bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-fix-ai-getting-stuck-on-geometry)). These are anecdotal.
4. **Teleport as the last resort** is Citizens' default (3.3).
5. **For K&K.** The L3 ladder already is the escalating pattern, with spacing and a cap, and stuck accounting per spell. What the field adds: (1) prevent most stucks upstream with clearance (3.4, 3.9), so the ladder is rare; (2) tell waiting from stuck (agents queued behind others); (3) an optional last step, a short teleport to the nearest free cell ahead on the path out of players' sight, which K&K has so far refused for good reasons (it looks wrong next to a player).

## 4. Best practices that carry over to a block grid

1. **Bake the body into the map.** A clearance value per walkable cell around the route, computed once per stretch by a distance transform, with a hard minimum of the body's width (one-block doors stay open) and a preferred margin used as a cost.
2. **Separate the layers.** Road graph (plan) → corridor and straightened line (string pulling on the clearance map) → short-horizon steering (optional) → Citizens (body).
3. **Aim at the farthest visible corner** of the corridor, not the next point; keep re-checking it as the NPC moves.
4. **Order beats physics at chokepoints.** Priorities and turn-taking avoid the doorway deadlocks that reciprocal avoidance suffers.
5. **Watchdog plus escalation**, and count waiting apart from stuck.
6. **Plan once per group; change the group's shape before the chokepoint**; let sub-groups pass in turn.
7. **Budget.** Do per-stretch work once and share it between NPCs on the same stretch; per-tick work only where the field shows it is needed.

## 5. Options for question 2 (steering)

All three keep the road graph, K&K's rules and the L3 ladder; all three use the same clearance map (best practice 1), so the corridor work is not lost whichever is chosen. They differ in who decides where the body goes between waypoints.

### Option A — Shape the line per stretch, Citizens walks it ("bake and pull")

1. **What.** For each ~32-block stretch, build a strip of cells around the road line, compute clearance by a distance transform, move the line to the clearance-preferred position, string-pull it where the strip is wide (plazas), align it through doorways, check every step with the body's width plus the margin; then hand the waypoints to Citizens as today (plan 4c.3 decision 1).
2. **From the field.** Recast's erosion and funnel, HAA*'s clearance, Theta*'s line of sight.
3. **Fixes.** Jagged walls, objects beside the road, entrance funnels, plaza lines: all static geometry. Not other NPCs (that is M4's turn-taking) and not anything that moves after the stretch was shaped.
4. **Cost.** Per stretch, shared: about 0.001 ms per NPC per tick amortised. No new engine port.
5. **Risk.** Low; it adds a step before today's waypoint handling, which stays as the safety net. The body still walks straight lines between waypoints, so a corner Citizens' body catches despite the margin is possible.
6. **Q3 and Q4 under A:** `corridor-half-width` 4 (the strip, enough for a 7-wide road; plazas widen it); `wall-clearance` 0.8 from the body's centre as a preferred margin, hard minimum 0.35 (body half width 0.3 plus 0.05), so one-block doors stay open.

### Option B — A plus a short-horizon K&K steering layer

1. **What.** Option A's shaped line, plus every few ticks (`steer-ticks`, proposed 4) each walking NPC aims at the farthest corner of its line it can see on the clearance map within a short horizon (3–4 blocks), adjusted by a context map (8–16 headings: interest along the line, danger from low clearance and from nearby NPCs in the occupancy hash), and Citizens gets a short waypoint list (2–3 points) towards it.
2. **From the field.** DetourCrowd's corridor with next corners, context steering (Game AI Pro 2), separation.
3. **Fixes.** Everything A fixes, plus things the shaped line can't foresee: an NPC that stopped in the way, a follower that strayed, oncoming traffic, a body that left the line after a turn.
4. **Cost.** Per NPC every few ticks: a handful of cached look-ups and the occupancy hash, estimated 0.003–0.008 ms per NPC per tick; plus Citizens' `setTarget` every few ticks, whose cost and smoothness (does the body hesitate on each new list?) must be measured in M1/M2 scenarios.
5. **Risk.** Medium: frequent re-targeting may make Citizens' movement jerky; needs a scenario with the measured cost before it ships. No new engine port (the existing waypoint list), but more calls into it.
6. **Q3 and Q4 under B:** `corridor-half-width` 4 (also the steering's map); `wall-clearance` 0.8 preferred, hard 0.35 (as A) for the line, and a danger margin of the same 0.8 in the context map; new `steer-ticks` 4 and `steer-horizon-blocks` 3.

### Option C — A K&K step controller (K&K steers the body every tick)

1. **What.** K&K computes the body's heading and speed every tick (path following with look-ahead, wall avoidance against the clearance map, separation, possibly ORCA-like avoidance), and the engine only moves and animates the body. Already listed in Phase 7 as the fallback "if shaped lines aren't enough".
2. **From the field.** Reynolds' steering behaviours, Unreal's character movement with RVO, Detour's velocity selection.
3. **Fixes.** The most control: smooth curves instead of straight segments, stairs stepped deliberately, dynamic obstacles and other NPCs every tick.
4. **Cost.** Per NPC per tick: estimated 0.005–0.015 ms in K&K, plus whatever the body move costs in the engine. Within 0.03 ms only if kept simple.
5. **Risk.** High: a new mover port (a per-tick move or velocity), which Citizens' public API does not offer for walking NPCs as far as this research found (it offers targets); moving the body around Citizens' navigator means relying on its internals or Minecraft's movement code, which is a coupling the engine-neutrality rule wants to avoid. Stairs and jumps become K&K's problem. Needs a spike first.
6. **Q3 and Q4 under C:** `corridor-half-width` 3–4 (the map the controller steers against; formations still need the width); `wall-clearance` becomes a soft avoidance radius, 0.6–0.8, since the controller corrects every tick.

### Add-on D — Clearance-aware K&K search for local legs and recovery (any option)

1. **What.** Local legs (short trips, a follower's leg back, the ladder's local leg) search K&K's own walk grid (`core/roads/walk/WalkPathfinder`) with clearance per cell (HAA*-style, hard minimum the body, margin as cost) and smooth the result (Theta* or the funnel), instead of Citizens' A*; Citizens walks the result as a waypoint list.
2. **Why.** Today the ladder's local leg is Citizens' A*, which knows the body's box but no preferred margin, and a failed search comes back as `NO_PATH` after it ran on the main thread. K&K's own search knows K&K's rules and the clearance, and can run on captured cells.
3. **Cost and risk.** Low to medium; K&K already has the walk pathfinder and its access rules.

### 5.1 Decision matrix (for the developer; DESIGN § 4.4)

Weights: 3 = matters most. Scores 1 (poor) to 3 (good).

| Consideration | Weight | A: bake and pull | B: A + short-horizon steering | C: step controller |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Static geometry (jagged walls, objects, doorways, plazas) | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| Dynamic situations (NPCs in the way, oncoming, strays) | 2 | 1 (M4's turn-taking covers chokepoints) | 3 | 3 |
| Tick cost within 0.03 ms | 3 | 3 | 2 | 1 |
| Engine neutrality and coupling to Citizens | 3 | 3 | 3 | 1 |
| Risk and effort | 2 | 3 | 2 | 1 |
| Smooth look (game feel) | 2 | 2 | 2 | 3 |
| **Weighted total** (max 45) | | **39** | **38** | **29** |

Reading: A is the cheapest and safest and is the base the others build on; B adds the dynamic cases at a measurable cost; C has the most control but the highest cost and coupling. The research favours starting with A (with M4's turn-taking for crowds) and adding B only if the M1 scenarios show dynamic cases that A and M4 leave, but the choice is the developer's.
