## Working Title

The title of the game is "Knights and Kings". A medieval/fantasy MMO Action-Adventure game in Minecraft.

## Current status

The game is, when this document was created, in development. Details of the gameplay and scenery are still being designed. The initial release date of the first production version is set to 15 December 2020. Chances are that, due to slow progression, the release date has to be moved to a later date.

## Concept Statement

Knights and Kings takes place in a massive "open world" with a medieval/fantasy theme. Players can go on numerous adventures and quests to earn money and level up to eventually conquer all kingdoms, alongside their clan members and other warriors.

## Genre

The game is a mixture of genres. Mainly, it is an Action-Adventure game. Due to the fact that the game has a massive almost open world with 7 kingdoms and around 25 towns to discover, perform quests, and acquire unique items along the way. Players are all in the same world and can cross each other in the game. Also, players can work together or against each other. Thus, classifying the game as an MMO genre as well.

The game also has multiple aspects of an RPG as well. It contains skill-trees in players' personal skills, weapons and bodyguards.

Main inspirations for the game are "Lord of the Rings" (its setting, characters, and overall look-and-feel), "Game of Thrones" (same as the previous), "Grand theft auto 5" (the game's open world genre, and the way players perform missions, earn and spend their money) and lastly "Sid Meier's Civilization V" (its strategic style is seen in Knights and Kings in the clan- and town-ruling content).

## Target Audience

The typical player of Knights and Kings is someone with decent fantasy and a little imagination. Aged 13 years and older the player understands the more complex strategies of the game. The player likes movies like Lord of the Rings and/or Game of Thrones. It likes to build up his account and acquire better items over time. The desired ESRB rating is Teen (13 years and up), although some mild forms of simulated gambling and violence are present in the game.

## Concept Paragraph and Unique Selling Points

Knights and Kings basically has 2 game phases: "Early-game" and "Late-game".

A new player starts in a relatively small town. The player has 1000 coins in cash and no house or any property. The player will follow some tutorials in which he gathers armor, a weapon and some food. After that, the player can earn money and experience by selling gathered resources, completing quests, play minigames or explore the world. In addition to these sources of income, the player unlocks professions to perform specific quests and improve a skilltree. These professions are unlocked through completing initial quests. Professions come in quite useful for the "Late-game".

When a player has gathered enough experience, it will be promoted to the next title (level) with a total of 18 titles. Each title grants an increased salary of coins (cash, received every played hour).

When a player's title and consequently his financial status increases, the player improves his personal skills and can afford more expensive, better items and even a house. A house is mainly used to store items. When a player becomes better it can travel to more distant and dangerous parts of the world, with higher rewards.

When a player has eventually reached the highest title, the game-phase of this player turns to "Late-game". The player probably has a big house, lots of coins and good items, which makes only playing quests to earn more money increasingly less relevant and boring. That's where the player should shift its focus more to professions, clans and eventually ruling towns.

Clans can be created and joined by players. A clan can then conquer all clan castles in a town, which, if 100% occupied by the same clan makes the clan capable of ruling over that town. But a clan should fit a major condition: A clan needs to have all professions present among their members, otherwise it cannot fully rule a town.

If the clan fits all conditions and is capable of ruling towns, it can proceed to conquering all towns in the world. If a clan manages to conquer all towns in the world, they gain the ultimate victory, rewarding all members a premium price according to a few factors (such as contribution, playtime, time since joining the clan).

It is not designed/decided yet what happens when ultimate victory is achieved. Options are:
- Resetting all clans (like seasons from e.g. Fortnite)
- Start a whole new concept or storyline
- Disband the clan

# 1. Product design

## Concept

Knights and Kings is a role-playing game wherein the player lives in a world divided in 7 kingdoms with multiple larger and smaller towns. The player can rent a room or, in a later stage in the game buy a house in a town of choice. The towns contain various shops where the player can buy items such as food, armor, weapons and magical items. The player can also acquire these items by chance when having completed a quest or mission. The player needs coins to buy items or houses. These coins can be acquired by completing quests, missions, playing minigames and selling acquired items to shops.

The player moves up in ranks with a total of 17 ranks. These ranks are derived from the actual medieval social hierarchy. The player can be promoted to a higher rank when enough experience is gained, but the player can also be demoted to a lower rank when the player is behaving badly. Each promotion unlocks a set of new functions, possibilities, minigames or items (e.g. reaching rank 5 unlocks the possibility to buy houses). In addition to this, each promotion grants the player a skill-point, which can be spent on upgrading a selected skill from a list of personal skills.

The aim of Knights and Kings is to produce a fun, addictive and adventurous MMORPG-game with a medieval/fantasy theme in Minecraft.

## Player Experience and Game POV

As said before, the game consists roughly of two phases: "Early-game" and "Late-game". The game will be set around the year 1300 AD, where magic, legends and myths are widely assumed to be real.

The world is divided into 7 kingdoms with every kingdom being a different tribe (e.g. Human, Dwarves, Elves etc.). Each individual kingdom's territory is divided into provinces, with each province having a few towns with one province-capital. Every kingdom has a capital with a palace. The towns and kingdoms are connected with each other by roads and paths in a manner that aligns with the way it used to be in the middle ages.

In the middle ages, it was very dangerous to wander outside the protected areas of towns, especially at night. To get this feeling of unknown, dark and hostile wilderness, the roads meander through the landscape and forests, creating a sensation of almost utter loneliness for players travelling from one town to another.

Roads in the wilderness are therefore not illuminated by streetlights or lanterns, only the parts near or inside towns have some form of streetlights. Streets also contain random objects and small structures such as an occasional well, bridge, statue or other forms.

To contribute even more to this almost scary and vulnerable feeling a player should get from wandering in the wilderness are various hostile entities such as: bandits, witches and ghosts. These hostiles will mainly ambush and attack players. The risk here is that when a player dies, the items it held in his inventory will drop on the ground, risking losing the items to other players or server cleanup.

## Key moments

When a player joins, it has no residence and no items. It has the default title called Serf (slave), with 1000 coins cash in its inventory. The player is approached by an NPC offering the player to show what to do and how to play the game (series of tutorials). Each completed tutorial will grant the player some rewards, and when the player completes the complete introduction tutorial series it will be promoted to the first title.

The player should then engage in various activities to gain coins and experience:
- Performing quests for shops
- Perform daily assignments
- Complete achievements
- Participate in minigames
- Discover new locations
- Gather and sell resources and items

While doing these types of activities, the player will increase in title and in financial status. When the player reaches title 5, it unlocks a slot to own a house and a slot to own a shop — the player can now permanently (disregarding 6 months of inactivity) store his personal items, and the shop allows more quests and more coins. The player also gains a skillpoint with each promoted title, spendable across a set of 5 skills.

When the player reaches title 10, it unlocks an extra slot for owning a house and a shop, plus a special-skill scroll — opening it grants a random special-skill out of 7, each giving unique advantages.

At title 15, a third slot unlocks for both houses and shops, an extra daily-assignment slot, and — the real highlight — a purchasable **Keep**. Owning a Keep allows hiring personal bodyguards and soldiers for combat, but it's also vulnerable to incoming Sieges from other Keep-owners, opening up new minigames.

Once a player reaches the highest title (18), focus shifts from the above activities to clans and professions.

## Art and inspiration

Knights and Kings' main inspiration comes from movies and series like Lord of the Rings and Game of Thrones. The game should, in disregard of Minecraft's textures, get a medieval/fantasy look and feel. Players should not be surprised when fantasy monsters lurk around the corner. Most of the art and inspiration is available under each kingdom's elaboration chapter.

Structures in the world should consider basic physics but combine medieval and fantasy — random structures such as ruins, temples/shrines, dungeons, and troll-caves should appear in the wilderness to give the world a unique and fun twist.

## Resource-packs

To create a more unique experience of each kingdom, the game has multiple resource-packs sharing the same item textures (except select kingdom-related commodities/weapons), differing mainly in placed-block textures — so each kingdom's architecture and build style reads as visually distinct. Candidate pack: "Winthor medieval" (planetminecraft.com), noted for good stone/road-block textures but weaker currency-representing blocks (diamond/gold/emerald).

## Current Target Platform

Minecraft Java Edition. Players need a licensed Minecraft JE copy and a PC capable of running it.

## Competition

Wynncraft, Dyescape, Hegemony, Massivecraft — Minecraft MMORPG servers considered direct or adjacent competition.

## Monetization

Mojang's EULA restricts server monetization to donations (nothing in return), cosmetic sales (no gameplay advantage/disadvantage), and a whole-server entry fee. Initially, revenue is planned as a mixture of Pay-to-Win (in-game advantages: items, coin-boosters) and cosmetic packages, plus DLC-like map/update unlocks via a premium shop. Once the game reaches roughly 1000–2000 players, Pay-to-Win elements are meant to be gradually removed/suppressed (to avoid Mojang legal risk against EULA violations), leaving cosmetics (and DLCs) as the long-term revenue model.

# 2. Detailed & Game Systems Design

## Player objectives and progression

The player starts as a Serf (title 1), without home or possessions. Advancing through titles (Knight, Count, King, etc.) grants more hourly salary and access to better items. A quick NPC-led tutorial introduces a new player to the realm.

## Game world

Towns are designed with inspiration from the middle ages, varying by kingdom (unique architecture, craftsmanship, traits, specialty commodities/resources) and even varying between provinces within a kingdom. Regardless of kingdom, every town should contain at minimum:
- 6 residential structures (houses)
- 3 shops (at least 1 consumable-category)
- 2 resource production structures
- 1 warehouse
- 1 town square
- 1 well
- 1 random non-functional building

Towns are divided into **districts** — useful for navigation in larger towns, for scoping minigames (e.g. Hide and Seek to one or two districts rather than a whole large town), and for grouping wealth tiers of structures to target a specific financial player segment (titles 15–17 are generally very wealthy). Districts are less relevant for smaller towns.

Towns are positioned and resourced according to their geography — e.g. some towns are iron/wood-rich, others fish/wheat-rich — and each town targets a specific wealth/title range, visible in its structures' average tier, with some outliers for realism. Resource-producing structures outside the town proper connect back via paths.

## Default structures and buildings

**Shops** (by category): Blacksmith, Armory, Archery, Jeweler, Fishery, Butchery, Bakery, Grocery, Furniture, Witchery, Stables.

**Houses**: all player-purchasable residential structures.

**Warehouses**: storage/distribution structures.

**Resource-producers**: Lumberyard, Mines, Quarry, Farm, Fishing docks.

**Other**: Taverns, Gates, Keeps, Clan castles.

**Non-functional** (size/quantity scales with town size): Libraries, Town-halls, Markets, Arenas, Town-armory/garrison, Religious buildings, Economical banks, Ruins (wilderness), Prison (usually within town-armory/garrison), Palace (extended town-hall for capitals).

## Elaboration of towns

**Houses** — found only in towns; single-owner at a time, sellable to transfer. Improvable with purchased furniture; personal item storage safe from death-loss. Not enterable by non-owners/non-friends except burglars (planned feature: break-in and steal from poorly-protected items, requiring a window of at least 2 blocks height). Roughly 5 wealth tiers of housing, aligned to title-based financial classes and district wealth grouping.

**Shops** — sell items ranging from food to weapons; stock/prices vary with supply from warehouses. Players can sell matching-category items to shops. Shops can offer quests. Owning a shop grants passive income (paid every 12 hours, capped at 1x payout while offline) and access to exclusive quests.

Shop attributes: **Product range** (low-end shops sell fewer/lower-grade items; high-end shops sell more, higher-grade items, in shop-levels), **Local storage**, **Sales profit**, **Crafting orders**, and **Levels** (20 levels per shop, gated by donated resources or completed quests; starting level tied to district wealth tier; capped at +5 levels above starting level to prevent long-term wealth-tier drift).

**Warehouses** — town-owned storage/distribution hubs; collect and distribute resources to shops, enable inter-town trade agreements, and accept player item sales. Unmanaged, they distribute stock evenly by predefined percentage; once a clan rules the town, the clan sets distribution percentages in weekly councils.

### Currency

**Coins** (main currency) — represented physically by gold nuggets (1), ingots (64), and blocks (4096); convertible at a bank in full-stack (64) increments. Spendable on items, room rentals, structures (houses/shops/keeps), enchantments, and repairs. Earnable via title salary/promotion, shop income, quests, daily assignments, minigames, treasures, and discovering new regions/villages/towns. Carrying coins risks loss on death; personal chests risk theft; a bank-rented personal vault avoids both.

**Gems** (special currency) — not a physical item, cannot buy Coins-section objects; used for loot boxes, extra quest/assignment slots, extra house/shop slots, cosmetics, and skill/special-skill removal. Never lost to death or theft; earned via quests, assignments, minigames, treasures, discovery, real-money webshop purchase, or completing a referral code's requirements.

### Combat

Combat between players only happens outside towns/villages by default (except in minigames/quests/assignments that explicitly allow it), and never between friends outside those activities. Being hit puts a player "In Combat" (can't log off for a set time; clears after 10s out of combat, or immediately on entering a town). Logging off while In Combat ("Combat Logging") drops all inventory items into the nearest village/town's warehouse, later purchasable on the market by anyone including the original owner.

On any death, the full inventory drops except items tagged **Soulbound** (destroyed on death) or **Ghosted** (retained, only droppable by explicit player action). Activities that explicitly say inventory isn't dropped (e.g. Siege, Hide and Seek) are the exception to full-drop-on-death.

Health/attack damage are further modified by Skills, Special Skills, and equipped weapons/armor.

### Skills

5 skills, 7 levels each, highest level unlocking an extra improvement: Strength, Speed, Health, Attack speed, Defense.

### Ideas and Expansions

**Professions** — 4 total, unlocked via special quests/tasks, each with clan-relevant benefits:
- **Trademaster** (starts as Transporter) — needed by clans to form trade agreements when ruling a town.
- **General** (starts as City guard).
- **Master of the Treasury** (starts as Salesman).
- **Master spy** (starts as Thief).

## Ruling a town

*(Dutch in source)* A town can be ruled by a clan once that clan controls 100% of the town's clan castles. Once ruling, the clan can decide: the percentage of stock allocated to eligible shops, tax rate, bank interest rate (paid from the town treasury), and propose/accept/reject/counter trade agreements (7-day terms). These decisions join the clan's weekly proposal list, except trade agreements, which can be proposed/accepted/rejected/adjusted at any time during the week; once two towns agree, the terms run for the following 7 days.

## Clans

*(Dutch in source)* Clans matter for late-game players. A player creates a clan by buying an empty clan castle (money + resources), then can invite players or accept join requests. Clans have 4 ranks: Leider (Leader), Co-leider (Co-leader), Lid (Member), Nieuweling (Newcomer) — relevant to permissions and weekly proposal voting. A clan becomes eligible to rule a town once it has 5 members.

## Kingdoms

Five kingdoms detailed (of a planned 7): **Hobbit** (red), **Men** (yellow), **Dragonborn** (orange), **Elves** (dark blue), **Dwarves** (green) — each with a background/specifications/specialty/famous-names/towns structure, and (for Men) explicit real-world/fictional inspiration links (Tolkien's Men of Gondor, House Lannister, medieval England, named real castles).

Famous names and starting towns per kingdom are catalogued but treated as early placeholders (many "Background"/"Specifications"/"Specialty" fields are empty in the source).
